MathMenace · number puzzle game

Privacy policy

Last updated October 11, 2026

In short: MathMenace doesn't collect, send or share any information about you. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or trackers. The puzzle you're in the middle of, and your fastest times, are kept on your own device so you can pick the game up again and see how you've done, and nowhere else.

What the app keeps, and where

MathMenace keeps six things, all about the game and none about you:

  • The puzzle you're playing (its numbers, which equations you've found, and how long you've played), so that you can leave and come back to it.
  • Your settings: the color palette, whether the game is light, dark or follows your phone, whether sound and buzzing are on, and whether you have been through the how-to-play practice, so the game is the way you left it.
  • Your Top Scores: for each puzzle you finish, how long it took, the points it scored, the grid size and the date, so the Top Scores page can show your fastest ones. The fastest ten for each grid size are kept.
  • Your daily puzzles: which days you finished the daily puzzle and how long each took, so the game can show a streak.
  • Your statistics: how many puzzles you have finished or given up on, the equations found and points scored, and your best and average time at each grid size, for the Statistics page.
  • Whether you have the full game: a note that the one-time purchase was made, so the game opens unlocked even without a connection.

It keeps nothing else. These stay on your device:

  • Android app: in the app's private storage on your phone. If you have Android's device backup turned on, Android may include it in your phone's backup to your Google account, as it does for most apps. That backup is handled by Google under your Google account settings; the game's developer can't see it.
  • iPhone app: in the app's private storage, which iOS may include in your iCloud or computer backups under your Apple account settings.
  • Browser version: in your browser's local storage for the game's page.

The saved puzzle is deleted when you finish it, give up, or start a new one. Your Top Scores stay until you clear them (there's a Clear scores button on the Top Scores page), and your look stays until you change it. Uninstalling the app deletes everything it kept.

What the app doesn't do

  • It doesn't ask for or collect your name, email address, location, contacts, photos or any other personal information.
  • It doesn't use advertising, analytics, crash reporting or tracking services, and it doesn't share or sell anything. The Share button hands a short message (your time, the grid size and a puzzle code) to your phone's own share sheet, and sends it nowhere unless you choose where.
  • The game itself runs entirely on your device and needs no internet. The only time the phone apps talk to anyone is to ask Google Play or the App Store about the full game purchase (its price, and whether you own it). The store handles the payment under your account with them: MathMenace never sees your payment details, and learns only whether the purchase was made.
  • The Android app declares the permissions Google's purchase component needs: to make purchases, to use the internet, and to see whether you're connected. The game itself doesn't use the internet; only that purchase component does, to reach Google Play.

Children

MathMenace collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If a future version changes any of this, for example by adding an online feature, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date above will change.
